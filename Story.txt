The story we’re telling is about a student who gets stuck between two lines of algebra and wants to understand how to keep going.

They’re doing homework. The first line of an equation makes sense. The next line looks different, and they wonder, “Where did the 3 go?” They find the topic in their AlgebraCards deck, follow the worked example, and listen to the explanation if they need it. The moment that matters is when they can say, “Oh, they divided both sides by 3. I can try that on my problem.”

That experience has a human origin. Farnaz Ehtessabian developed AlgebraCards in 2013 while teaching algebra and used them in her own classes. Her story shows where the method came from. A genuine review then gives another person’s account of what it was like to use the cards.

By the time visitors reach the shop, they’ve seen what it feels like to use a card. A student can recognize a topic they need help with. A parent can picture the deck on the table during homework. Choosing a set becomes a natural next step.

That’s also why the footer quote belongs. By the time visitors read “involve me and I may understand,” they’ve already seen what involvement looks like: the student works out the next step themselves.
